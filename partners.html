For schools, counsellors & agents

A route into Europe you can put your name to.

If you advise students in India, China, the Philippines, Nigeria, Egypt or the Gulf, you already know the problem: families want Europe, cannot fund the UK, and are surrounded by advisers who will say anything to close. We are proposing the opposite.

The proposition

Your reputation is the asset.
We treat it that way.

A counsellor's value compounds over years and can be destroyed by one badly placed student whose family talks. So the only referral relationship worth having is one where the downside risk is managed as carefully as the upside.

We will tell your student no when no is the right answer, we will document every figure we quote them, and we will tell you at the outset which institutions pay us a commission. If that makes a particular placement less likely, we accept it.

What we ask in return is equally simple: send us students who are genuinely considering Greece, not students you are trying to place somewhere because nothing else worked.

What you get

Accurate, current information

Programme lists, awarding bodies, published fees and entry requirements — with sources, so you can verify before you advise.

What you get

A named contact, not a portal

One advisor who answers, knows your market, and will tell you honestly when a student's file is not going to clear a consulate.

What you get

Visibility through the process

You are kept informed at application, offer, visa and arrival, so you are never the last person to learn what happened to your student.

Where this works best

The student profile that
Greece genuinely suits.

Referrals that fit this profile convert well and finish their degrees. Referrals that do not, fail expensively for everyone involved — including you.

Strong fit

  • — Wants a UK or US degree, cannot fund UK or US tuition
  • — English at or near IELTS 6.0, or willing to prepare for it
  • — Family can evidence funds of roughly €12,000–€19,000 for year one
  • — Business, computing, engineering, hospitality or psychology
  • — Values an EU base and is not banking on a post-study work visa

Poor fit — please do not send these

  • — Needs a top-100 ranked research university
  • — Primary goal is a long post-study work visa or migration route
  • — Cannot evidence funds and is relying on part-time work to cover tuition
  • — Entering a regulated profession without checking home-country registration
  • — Needs to start in under four months with documents not yet begun

How a partnership runs

Four stages, documented.

No exclusivity demands, no volume targets, no pressure to push a particular institution. If it stops working for your students, it should stop.

01

Week one

An introductory call

Your market, your typical student profile, and an honest assessment of whether Greek routes will serve them. Some of these calls end with us saying this is not a fit.

02

Before any referral

Terms in writing

Referral arrangement, commission structure where one applies, data-handling obligations, and what each side commits to. In writing, before a single student is introduced.

03

Ongoing

Materials and briefings

Current programme and fee information for your counsellors, and a briefing whenever an institution changes fees, partners or entry requirements.

04

Every intake

Outcome reporting

What happened to the students you sent: offers, visa outcomes, enrolments, and where things went wrong. You cannot advise well next year without knowing this year's results.

Open a conversation

Let's see whether this fits your market.

Tell us where you are based, roughly how many students you advise each year, and what they are currently choosing instead. We will tell you candidly whether a Greek route is worth adding.
