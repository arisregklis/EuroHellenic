Line by line

Where the money actually goes

Monthly ranges for a student living normally — not austerely, and not expensively. Athens sits at the top of each range; Thessaloniki, Patras and the island cities sit below it.

The variable that matters most

Rent

€300–€450 for a room in a shared flat in a regional city.
€450–€700 for a shared flat or small studio in Athens.

Rent and utilities together are what separate a €700 month from a €1,400 month. Everything else is comparatively stable.

Groceries and eating out

Food

€180–€280 groceries.
€60–€150 eating out and coffee.

Greek supermarkets and local markets are inexpensive by western European standards. The social coffee culture is real and it is a budget line.

Getting around

Transport

€15–€35 a month for a student pass, after the student discount of up to 50%.

Athens has metro, bus, tram and suburban rail. Most students in central districts walk more than they ride.

Bills

Utilities

€60–€130 for electricity, water and heating.

Winter heating is the spike. Older Athenian apartments can be poorly insulated — ask about heating type before you sign.

Connectivity

Mobile & internet

€20–€35 combined.

Cheap and straightforward. You will need a Greek number for almost every administrative step, so arrange it in week one.

A life, not just a degree

Personal & social

€60–€150 for books, clothes, going out and travel.

Budget it deliberately. Students who plan zero social spending are the ones who quietly overspend and then panic in March.

Do not forget

The costs nobody quotes

Rental deposit (commonly one to two months), health insurance, residence permit fee, document translation and apostille, flights, and a first-month buffer before any income starts. Budget €1,500–€2,500 on top of month one.

The national D visa

Paperwork, in the order
you should do it.

Student visa applications are rarely refused on academic grounds. They are refused for incomplete files, weak proof of funds, and documents that were never translated or apostilled. Start early and be pedantic.

01

Before anything else

Secure your acceptance letter

Nothing else in this process can begin without a formal offer from the institution. Everything downstream — funds, insurance, appointment — references it.

02

Start now — this one has a lead time

Criminal record certificate, apostilled and translated

Issued by your home authority, legalised with an apostille, then officially translated. In several countries this alone takes four to eight weeks. It is the most common reason a file misses an intake.

03

The stage that decides the outcome

Proof of sufficient funds

The required monthly amount is set annually against Greek living standards, separate from tuition. Acceptable evidence includes personal bank statements, a scholarship award (IKY, Onassis or institutional), or a sponsor letter with full supporting documentation. Present comfortably above the minimum. Do not move a large sum into the account the week before — consulates read statement history, not just balances.

04

A frequent refusal reason

Health insurance valid in Greece

Must cover hospital and medical treatment for the entire duration of the visa and the subsequent residence permit. Check the wording of the policy against the consulate's requirement, not against what the broker tells you it covers.

05

Assemble and submit

Passport, photos, accommodation, application

Passport valid for the whole study period, photographs to the stated specification, proof of accommodation in Greece, and the completed application lodged at the Greek consulate with jurisdiction over where you live.

06

Allow one to three months

Decision — then book flights

From a complete application, plan for one to three months to a decision. Do not book non-refundable travel before approval. Every year, students do, and every year some of them lose the money.

07

Your first weeks in Greece

The residence permit

Apply for the ádeia diamonís at the local Aliens and Immigration Department soon after arrival, with your passport, D visa, acceptance letter, proof of funds, insurance and accommodation evidence. Then register for AFM (tax number) and AMKA (social security) — you cannot legally work, and often cannot rent properly, without them.

Working while you study

Up to 20 hours per week during term time, with a valid residence permit, AFM and AMKA, in formally registered employment. Undeclared work puts both your permit and your wage protection at risk. Full-time work may be possible during official holidays — confirm with the Migration Office. Migration authorities do monitor academic progress at renewal, so work that visibly delays your degree creates a problem later.

Renewals and staying on

Residence permits are renewed against continued enrolment and demonstrated academic progress. Greece does not operate a long post-study work route comparable to the UK Graduate Route. Remaining after graduation generally requires a job offer with a corresponding permit, or progression to further study. Build that into your plan in year one, not in your final semester.
